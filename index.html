The Queen City Community Guide

Neighborhood reporting by Alisena Nasirali, community correspondent

Why This Guide Exists

Charlotte grows by roughly a hundred people a day, and most of them arrive knowing where the airport is and very little else. This guide is my attempt to fix that one event at a time. I walk the grounds, talk to the people running the booths, and write down the practical things nobody puts on a flyer: where to actually park, which entrance is faster, and whether the food is worth the line.

What I Cover

  • Free and low-cost events that welcome families
  • Long-running traditions that shaped the neighborhoods around them
  • Practical logistics such as parking, transit, and arrival timing
  • Local artists, musicians, and food vendors worth seeking out

This Month's Community Spotlight

Every September, Freedom Park fills with tents, stages, and the smell of funnel cake for a weekend that has been running since the Johnson administration. It is free, it is enormous, and it is the closest thing Charlotte has to a citywide reunion. I spent all three days there taking notes so you can plan a better visit than my first one.

How I Choose What to Feature

I look for events that are genuinely open to everyone. That usually means free or cheap admission, a location reachable without a car, and an atmosphere where nobody feels out of place for showing up alone. If an event only works for people who already know somebody there, it does not make the guide.
